How the numbers work

Everything here comes from company filings, earnings calls and reported deals up to 29 September 2026. Headline amounts are as announced and often overlap, so the map total is a measure of ambition, not a sum of new money.

Builders (they own the data centers)

capital = capex 2024 + 2025 + 2026 guide × share serving the revenue line needed / yr = capital × CRF(return, years) ÷ cash margin CRF(r, n) = r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n) (1 ÷ n when r = 0) today = revenue run-rate now − 2023 revenue, × credit share

Revenue lines: Microsoft Cloud, all of Alphabet, AWS, all of Meta, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, and all revenue for CoreWeave and SpaceXAI's AI segment. Run-rates annualize the latest quarter. "Asset lives" mode splits capital 60/40 between servers (5 years) and buildings and power (20 years), in line with Alphabet's disclosed mix.

Labs (they rent compute)

needed / yr = average yearly compute commitment, 2026–2030 ÷ compute share of revenue

OpenAI: about $600B of compute through 2030, its own February 2026 target, spread evenly over five years. Anthropic: the $518B in its IPO filing, each contract spread over its stated term (the Broadcom leases are assumed to run 2027–2033 and AMD 2027–2030). That averages $68B a year for 2026–2030, peaking near $83B in 2027–29.

Verdicts

  • Leading: needs no more than 1.5× today's revenue and funds its 2026 spending from operating cash flow.
  • On track: needs no more than 2×.
  • Must keep innovating: needs 2–3.5×, which means growing roughly 18–34% a year to 2030.
  • Needs a breakthrough: needs more than 3.5×.

Growth needed = (needed ÷ today)^(1 ÷ 4.25) − 1, from October 2026 to the end of 2030. "Spend ÷ cash flow" divides 2026 capital spending by the last twelve months of operating cash flow; CoreWeave and SpaceXAI use adjusted EBITDA, and for the labs it is the compute bill divided by revenue.

Cross-checks

J.P. Morgan estimates the industry needs about $650B of annual revenue for a 10% return on its modeled build-out through 2030; Bain puts the revenue needed by 2030 at $2 trillion. Goldman Sachs expects the five biggest US hyperscalers to spend $1.2 trillion on AI in 2027. The default settings here land in the same range.

Keeping it current

New developments are logged in one data file shared with the Supply Chain Atlas, from the Update desk on the live site or on GitHub; an entry can change a project's status or add a new project. On the live site, share prices and analyst targets refresh from Yahoo Finance every 15 minutes on weekdays, and the Update desk can refresh them at any time. Capex, revenue and backlog figures are updated after each earnings season, and the payback test reruns on the new numbers.

Caveats

  • Headline figures mix different things: capex, multi-year pledges, contract values, bond authorizations and credit guarantees. Each is labeled in the table.
  • Builders' "today" is generous by default: it credits all growth since 2023 to the new capex. Lower the credit slider to see who depends on that.
  • Off-balance-sheet leases (Meta's Hyperion and El Paso ventures, Microsoft's neocloud contracts) are not in the capital figures. Neither is 2027 spending, which is guided higher almost everywhere.
  • Private-company revenue is reported, not audited. Anthropic reports part of its revenue on a gross basis.
  • Non-dollar amounts are converted at rates near the announcement date.
  • This is a research model, not investment advice.
