Where it's made

Plants, mines and campuses behind each link. The path you traced above stays lit; everything else dims. Solid red rings mark sites struck, offline or threatened; dashed rings mark sites that are short, tight or under export control.

Who holds the chokepoints

A chokepoint here is a link where one company, a few companies or one country controls most of the supply and a substitute takes years. They are rated by how tight supply is today, not by how bad a failure would be; the worst cases below cover that.

Where the chokepoints sit

Chokepoints by the country that controls them. Japan holds more than any other country, mostly in materials few people have heard of.

How to read the ratings

    When it breaks

    Ten ways the chain fails, from what is happening now to the worst case. Each supply shock runs through a weakest-link model of the chain: a link can only deliver what its scarcest critical input allows. The percentages are the share of planned supply still delivered, not forecasts.

    The watchlist

    Public companies at each link, with the latest price, the average analyst price target and the projection each one has to deliver. Prices are in local currency and dated, because markets trade on different days and hours.

    How much upside analysts still see

    Each dot is a company: the gap between its price and the average analyst target. The dark tick is each group's median. Select a dot to find it in the table.

    What's changed

          How this was built

          The chain

          Sixty-four links in eleven stages, from mining to paying customers. A link is a market (such as ABF film or gas turbines), not a company. Each link lists its main makers and their shares where a source gives one; where a source only says a firm dominates, the page says that instead of inventing a number. Dashed lines are minor inputs that are shown but left out of the stress model.

          Chokepoint ratings

          Critical: short today and no substitute within a year. Serious: tight, held by one or two firms, or under a live or scheduled export control. Watch: highly concentrated but supplying normally. The ratings are this page's judgment from the cited reporting.

          The stress model

          Each link delivers the smaller of its own remaining capacity and what its scarcest critical input allows. Some inputs pass only part of a shortfall through, because fabs hold inventory, get priority allocation or can recycle (helium into fabs passes 10%; tools into fabs 25%, since installed tools keep running). Substitutes sit in shared groups: merchant GPUs and custom chips supply servers 72:28, matching ASIC servers' 27.8% share of 2026 shipments.

          delivered(link) = min( 1 − own shock, min over input groups( Σ w·(1 − pass·(1 − delivered(input))) / Σ w ) )

          Shocks are set per scenario for the first 12 months and for year three, and are listed under each scenario. "The money stops" is a demand shock: it cuts AI orders by 25% then 40% and scales each link's loss by how much of its sales go to AI (high 0.8, medium 0.4, low 0.1, a judgment).

          Prices and forecasts

          Last close, average analyst target, rating and forecasts come from Stock Analysis (S&P Global consensus), accessed 30 Sep 2026. Dates differ because of holidays (Korea's Chuseok, Taiwan's Mid-Autumn Festival) and page refresh lags; each row shows its own date. Tokyo Electron's published target was not split-adjusted, so it is left out. Nothing here is investment advice.

          Limits

          Market shares come from different years and definitions. The model treats each link as one pool and ignores prices, stockpiles beyond the pass-through factors, and demand falling when supply falls. Map pins are city-level.
