How it works

Thousands of futures

Each future decides every yes-or-no question with the chance you set and draws every quantity from the range you set, letting linked ones move together. What happens is then applied to the chain, with the same weakest-link model as the worst cases in The Chain, and to buyers' orders. The page records what gets built in each future and counts.

The same 10,000 futures are reused every time, so a change in the results comes from your numbers and never from luck.

Reading the numbers

  • A chance is how likely the answer to the question is yes, by its deadline.
  • For a quantity, low is the level undercut 1 time in 10, central has an even chance either side, and high is beaten 1 time in 10.
  • The headline is the average of all the futures, so it moves when something rare but severe becomes more likely. The typical future beside it barely moves in that case, which is why both are shown.
  • How bad, if it happens scales an event's effect: 1 is the standard case, 0.5 half of it.
  • Suppose keeps only the futures in which the thing happens. Everything linked to it shifts, and each affected row shows where it lands.
  • A link runs from −0.95 (opposite ways) through 0 (unrelated) to +0.95 (same way). The five words set −0.6 (much less likely, or much lower), −0.3, 0 (no link), +0.3 and +0.6; Fine-tune sets anything in between. For a quantity, "happens" means it hits its high case or better, a 1-in-10 outcome. Each thing keeps its own chance whatever its links are; links only change what tends to happen together.
  • How strongly things move together multiplies every link at once: none, half, as set, or 1.5 times as strong. Where links that strong cannot all be true at once, the futures use the closest set that can.

What this leaves out

  • Stockpiles and delivery times. A shortfall cuts what is built in the same year.
  • Timing detail. An event is equally likely to start on any day between now, or its earliest possible date, and its deadline.
  • Supply above the plan. A link that delivers more than planned is counted as delivering the plan.
  • Workarounds. A shortfall in a scarce input passes down the chain exactly as in The Chain's stress model.
